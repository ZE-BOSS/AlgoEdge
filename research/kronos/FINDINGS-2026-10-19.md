# Kronos: research findings and backtest results

*19 Oct 2026. Everything below was measured on this PC against FundedNext MT5 data. The code is in `backend/kronos_service/`; the plan is in [IMPLEMENTATION_PLAN.md](IMPLEMENTATION_PLAN.md); raw results are in `research/kronos/results/*.json`.*

## The answer first

**Kronos works as advertised technically. On almost everything you can trade it gave no usable signal, with one exception worth forward-testing: the S&P 500 (SPX500) on H1.**

I tested 12 symbols, all **after** its training data ends (June 2024): 7 US stocks including SpaceX, Nasdaq 100, S&P 500, gold and EURUSD. The results:
- **Its direction call was never statistically significant.** No symbol reached |t| > 1.7 (2.0 is the usual bar).
- **Simply being long beat it** on 4 of 7 stocks.
- **Plain momentum beat it on gold** (t 2.8, against Kronos's t 0.5).
- **As a gate on ORB it doesn't select:** the setups it rejected did as well as the ones it kept.
- **Its probability isn't calibrated.** When it says "10% chance up", the stock rose about half the time.
- **Its volatility forecast loses to "the same hours yesterday"** on 9 of 10 symbols.

**The exception: SPX500 on H1.** Traded as a strategy in app form (take the direction when 4–5 of its 5 sampled paths agree, hold 10 hours, 2×ATR stop, real spread **and swap**), it made:
- **+0.152R per trade over 440 trades** (t 2.77, PF 1.39, max drawdown 10.4R), Feb 2025 → Oct 2026;
- positive in **both** independent halves (+0.13R and +0.18R);
- better than **all 30** random-direction runs on the same entry bars.

But it's the best of 12 symbols tested, so after correcting for that it's borderline (p ≈ 0.07). The closely related NDX100 doesn't show it, and US30 shows only a weak version.

**So:** don't wire Kronos into live trading as a gate. **Do forward-test the SPX500 H1 setup** (section 6b) before risking money. Recommendation 3 in section 9 describes the one other test that matches how Kronos's authors actually made money with it.

## 1. What Kronos is, and what its paper actually showed

- **What it is:** a decoder-only transformer over tokenised candlesticks (OHLCV). Each bar becomes two 10-bit tokens, and the model predicts the next bar's tokens.
- **Training data:** 12 billion+ K-lines from 45 exchanges, mostly equities, plus crypto, futures and forex, at frequencies from 1 minute to weekly. It ends **June 2024**. MIT licensed. Paper at AAAI 2026 ([arXiv 2508.02739](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.02739v1)); code on [GitHub](https://github.com/shiyu-coder/Kronos).
- **Open models:** mini (4.1M params, context 2048), small (24.7M, 512) and base (102M, 512). Large isn't released.

**What the paper's numbers mean.** The headline is "+93% RankIC over other foundation models". In absolute terms Kronos's correlation between forecast and outcome is small:

| Market (paper, Kronos-small) | Price IC | Return IC |
|---|---|---|
| Shanghai A-shares | 0.055 | — |
| **NASDAQ (XNAS)** | **0.034** | **0.056** |
| Forex | 0.028 | 0.040 |
| Crypto | 0.025 | 0.037 |

An IC of 0.03 is a real but tiny edge. It's usable when you rank **hundreds of stocks** at once and hold the top 50, and that is exactly how the paper's only money result was made:
- a **daily, long-only, top-50 portfolio of China A-shares** (CSI 300 and CSI 800);
- 0.15% costs, 5-day minimum hold;
- **+18% annualised excess return** (Kronos-small).

There's no published result for single-instrument timing, intraday bars, CFDs, or anything you can short.

**Independent evidence is thin.** One public test, Kronos-small on 5-minute BTC, out of sample, found its probabilities **indistinguishable from a random-walk model** (Brier 0.189 against 0.188). I found no independent stock or forex test.

## 3. How it was tested

- **Model:** Kronos-mini, 512-bar lookback, 12-bar horizon, 5 sampled paths, temperature 1.0, top-p 0.9. Small is 2.4× slower; with no edge from mini, there was no case for spending three times the compute on small.
- **Forecast skill:** non-overlapping 12-bar forecasts from the last closed bar.
  - **Kronos:** trade in the direction of its expected move, close to close, paying the measured spread.
  - **"Confident":** trade only when at least 80% or at most 20% of paths end higher.
  - **Baselines:** **momentum** (sign of the last 12 bars) and **always long**.
- **Gate value:** ORB_v1 and TrendBreakout_v1 setups, through the app's own `BacktestEngine` with its costs and risk layer. Run three ways: all setups, the setups Kronos agrees with, and the setups it **rejects**. The rejected set is the control: a gate that knows something leaves its rejected trades clearly worse.
- **No lookahead:** a test rewrites every bar after the decision bar and checks the features don't change. My inference matches upstream's code to within 2×10⁻⁵.

## 4. Results: direction

**H1 bars, 12-hour horizon.** Stocks: ~273 forecasts each, Nov 2024 → Oct 2026. NDX100, SPX500, XAUUSD, EURUSD: 400 forecasts each, Dec 2025 → Oct 2026. Net bp is per trade, after spread.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| AAPL | 51.3% | +7.8 (0.54) | +31.2 (1.56) | −1.1 | +17.5 (1.21) | +0.046 |
| NVDA | 48.7% | +23.8 (1.06) | +30.9 (1.00) | −6.9 | +23.1 (1.03) | +0.020 |
| TSLA | 56.0% | +43.1 (**1.62**) | +13.0 (0.40) | −40.3 (−1.51) | +11.6 | +0.070 |
| MSFT | 45.8% | −8.6 (−0.56) | −6.7 | −7.0 | +12.2 (0.79) | −0.040 |
| AMZN | 44.9% | −4.9 (−0.27) | +14.7 | −21.5 (−1.19) | +12.2 | −0.004 |
| META | 46.4% | +5.6 (0.28) | −3.9 | −12.3 | +15.7 (0.78) | +0.002 |
| **NDX100** | 49.8% | +2.7 (0.58) | −0.9 | −3.0 | +3.4 (0.74) | −0.006 |
| **SPX500** | **57.3%** | **+9.8 (3.39)** | **+14.6 (3.54)** | +2.9 (0.98) | +2.2 (0.75) | **+0.190** |

**What this says:**
- **Across the 6 large caps, Kronos averaged about +11 bp per trade and always-long about +15 bp.** The model mostly picked up the bull market. It isn't significant on any single stock.
- **Its "confident" calls aren't better in any consistent way:** better on AAPL and AMZN, worse on TSLA and META.
- **Rank IC averages about +0.01 on stocks.** That's the paper's order of magnitude (0.03–0.06), but with a few hundred forecasts it can't be told apart from zero. Also, the paper's figure is a *cross-sectional* IC across many stocks at once, not a timing IC on one stock.

**Calibration fails.** The table shows, for each band of Kronos's `p_up`, how often the stock actually rose:

| Kronos said | AAPL | NVDA | TSLA | MSFT | AMZN | META | NDX100 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ≤ 20% up | 54% | 61% | 46% | 57% | 52% | 55% | 61% |
| ≥ 80% up | 59% | 59% | 59% | 58% | 55% | 49% | 53% |

A forecaster with skill shows a rising staircase. This one is flat at roughly the base rate. So a gate rule like "only buy if p_up ≥ 0.6" filters on something close to noise.

## 5. Results: SpaceX, a stock Kronos has never seen

SPCX listed in June 2026. Test: M15 bars, 12-bar (3-hour) horizon, 139 forecasts, mid-Jul → mid-Oct 2026.

| | Net per trade (t) | Hit rate |
|---|---|---|
| Kronos | **+18.6 bp (0.74)** | 57.6% |
| Momentum | **−115 bp (−4.57)** | |
| Always long | −35 bp | |

- **Kronos's best rank IC of any symbol: +0.157.**
- **But the dominant effect is mean reversion:** a new stock's moves reverse, so momentum loses heavily and *fading* the last move earns about +19 bp after costs, the same as Kronos and without the model.
- **The 48 bp spread eats most of any edge,** and this is 3 months of one stock.
- **Verdict:** interesting, not tradable. It's worth re-checking when SPCX has a year of history and a normal spread.

## 6. Results: as a gate on your strategies

**M5 bars, Jun 2025 → Oct 2026, through the app's `BacktestEngine`.**

| Strategy | Gate threshold | All setups | Kronos agrees (kept) | Kronos disagrees (rejected) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| XAUUSD ORB | 60% of paths agree | 268 tr, +0.071R | 146 tr, +0.076R | 135 tr, **+0.079R** |
| XAUUSD ORB | 80% | | 66 tr, +0.072R | 209 tr, **+0.091R** |
| NDX100 ORB | 60% | 293 tr, +0.006R | 136 tr, **+0.070R** | 169 tr, −0.047R |
| NDX100 ORB | 80% | | 80 tr, +0.032R | 229 tr, +0.031R |
| NDX100 Trend Breakout | | only 1 trade on M5: inconclusive | | |
| XAUUSD Trend Breakout | | only 3 trades on M5: TB is a slow strategy, so this is inconclusive | | |

Kept and rejected trades don't add up to the total, because removing a trade frees a position slot for another one.

- **On gold the gate has no value.** At the stricter threshold, the trades it rejected did *better* than the ones it kept.
- **NDX100 at 60% looks good:** +0.07R kept against −0.05R rejected.
  - But the gap has t ≈ 0.6.
  - It **vanishes at 80%**, where a real effect should get stronger.
  - It's one market among the several tested, which is what chance looks like.
- **Promotion rule from the plan** (rejected half clearly worse, ≥ 100 trades each side, out of sample): **nothing passes.**

## 6b. Results: Kronos traded as a strategy (app form)

**What I ran:** `backend/kronos_service/strategy_test.py`, through the app's `BacktestEngine`.
- **Entry:** the next H1 open after the decision bar.
- **Stop:** 2 × ATR(14).
- **Target:** 6R, which is rarely hit; trades end on time.
- **Exit:** after 10 bars.
- **Costs:** the engine's measured spread and slippage, **plus overnight swap**.
- **Control:** random directions on exactly the same entry bars, repeated 30 times.

**Confident calls** means 4–5 of the 5 paths agree; **all calls** means the sign of the expected move.

**SPX500, split by period and threshold:**

| | Early: Feb → Dec 2025 | Recent: Dec 2025 → Oct 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| 4–5 of 5 paths agree | 227 tr, +0.129R, t 1.65, PF 1.32 | 213 tr, +0.176R, t 2.30, PF 1.47, max DD 6.2R |

**How to read it:**
- **The edge sits only in the confident calls**, and it's there in both periods. That's the pattern a real signal makes. Noise would usually show up in one period and vanish in the other.
- **Against it:**
  - SPX500 is the best of **12** symbols tested.
  - NDX100, about 90% correlated with SPX500, is *negative*.
  - The early period alone isn't significant.
- **About 1 confident trade a day** on average (440 in ~20 months). At +0.15R and 1% risk that's very roughly **+3% a month before compounding**, *if* it holds. That's a big "if" until forward testing confirms it.

## 7. Results: volatility

Volatility is the one place the paper claims a solid gain (9% lower MAE). Kronos's forecast range over the next 12 bars, against the realised range, as a Spearman correlation:

| | AAPL | AMZN | META | MSFT | NVDA | TSLA | NDX100 | EURUSD | XAUUSD | SPCX |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Kronos | 0.12 | 0.14 | 0.15 | 0.06 | 0.13 | 0.11 | 0.29 | 0.24 | **0.11** | 0.30 |
| "Same hours yesterday" | **0.50** | **0.37** | **0.39** | **0.40** | **0.40** | **0.54** | **0.42** | **0.39** | 0.10 | **0.68** |

**A one-line seasonal baseline beats Kronos on 9 of 10.** Most of what "will the next 12 hours be wide?" depends on is *which* 12 hours: US cash session or overnight. Kronos gets the timestamps, but it uses them far less well than simply looking at yesterday.

- **It's runnable, but there's nothing yet worth running.**

## 9. Recommendations

1. **Don't enable a Kronos gate or Kronos RL features on any live slot.** Nothing passed the promotion rule, and the gate on gold ORB was worse than none.
2. **Forward-test the SPX500 H1 "confident call" setup, on demo, before any money:**
   - **Rule:** at each H1 close, if ≥ 4 of 5 Kronos-mini paths agree on direction over the next 12 bars, enter at the next open. 2×ATR(14) stop, close after 10 bars.
   - **Scale:** about 1 trade a day.
   - **What counts as confirmation:** over ~150–200 forward trades, a mean of at least +0.08R with the random-direction control still beaten.
   - **What I can build next if you want it:** a live "shadow" runner (plan Phase 2a) that logs these calls each hour, so you can trade or paper-trade them on Deriv's "US 500" or FundedNext's SPX500.
3. **Keep the code.** It's isolated in `backend/kronos_service/`, needs no torch in the trading stack, and is tested. It costs nothing while disabled.
4. **The other honest test is to run it the paper's way.** Rank Deriv's ~450 US stocks daily by Kronos expected return, long the top decile, hold ≥ 5 days, and pay real CFD costs plus swap. That's the only setup with published evidence. It needs Deriv's daily stock history (log the Deriv terminal in on this PC), and it's roughly a day of CPU time at daily bars.
5. **Fine-tuning (Phase 3)** is the other route the authors suggest. Zero-shot showed signal on only one index, so I'd expect little from it, and it needs a GPU.
6. **Two side findings, both free and needing no model:**
   - **Momentum on gold at H1/12h:** +17 bp per trade net, t 2.8. It agrees with this project's earlier trend findings, and could be a filter for the gold slots.
   - **"Same hours yesterday" is a strong volatility forecast.** It's free, and better than Kronos.

## 10. What's untested, and the caveats

- **Kronos-small was only timed, not evaluated.** Given mini's results and the paper's small-vs-base differences (≤ 10% in IC), I'd expect the same verdict.
- **Swap is excluded** from the direction tests, which flatters every leg, Kronos included, on stocks.
- **The FundedNext stock sample is about 2 years.** Each symbol is one sample; the 7 stocks are correlated, so they aren't 7 independent tests.
- **ORB and Trend Breakout were the only strategies gated.** OvernightSession and the RL agents weren't.
