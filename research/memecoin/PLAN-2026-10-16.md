## 2. What the TikTok system actually is, piece by piece

| Their name | What it does | How we'd build it | Needs Grok? |
|---|---|---|---|
| CRAWLER / SCAN | Detects new tokens and "finds what moves" | Solana RPC websocket (Helius) for new pools on pump.fun, PumpSwap, Raydium and Meteora, plus GeckoTerminal/DexScreener polling as a backup | No |
| Kill list (`thresholds.py`: HARD / SOFT) | Rejects scam coins | Hard rules: mint/freeze authority, LP lock, top-10 holders, creator history, sell simulation (honeypot), minimum age and liquidity. Soft rules: momentum already spent, concentration rising | No |
| SOCIAL | "the project's X account", "recycled account" | Checks whether the token's X account exists, how old it is, whether it was renamed or recycled, and whether the attention is real or bots | **Yes**, or the paid X API |
| VET | Chain risk: "is it real", "dev still loaded" | Creator wallet's holdings and history over RPC | No |
| "Jev" | "Tiny AI judge, yes or no, under a cent" | A small model that scores the tokens that pass the hard rules. It could be a cheap LLM call, or a classifier trained on our own collected outcomes | Optional |
| CHIEF / SIZE / FILLS / RISK | Sizing, execution and closing | Jupiter swap API, or the pump.fun bonding curve, with Jito bundles for inclusion, fixed size, daily loss limit and automatic sell if liquidity is pulled | No |

**On Grok:** we have not used Grok anywhere in AlgoEdge. Its one capability nothing else here offers cheaply is **live search over X (Twitter)**, and meme coins live and die on X. So it is needed for the SOCIAL check. The alternative is the paid X API, which costs more and gives raw posts rather than an assessment. I'd use Grok only for the SOCIAL check and as one input to the judge, never to call direction. Past tests here found an LLM calling direction had no edge ([[algoedge-llm-direction-test-failed]] in my notes).

## 3. How I'll test it: four phases, each with a pass/fail bar set in advance

### Phase 1: collect data going forward (7–14 days, no trading)

1. Record every new pool: first block, creator, initial liquidity.
2. Take snapshots at about 0, 1, 5, 15 and 60 minutes after creation:
   - RugCheck report;
   - top holders over RPC;
   - creator wallet history;
   - liquidity and price.
3. Run the **Grok SOCIAL** check on the tokens that pass the hard rules (about 5–10%, so roughly 60–120 calls an hour), and store its answer and reasoning.
4. Record the price and liquidity path at 1-minute resolution for 24 hours, plus whether and when liquidity was pulled.

**Why forward and not historical:** free APIs only list *recent* new pools. A historical backfill (every token launched in the last 3–6 months, with paths) needs a paid source: Birdeye, Bitquery or Dune. If you're willing to pay for one, Phase 1 takes days instead of weeks, and the test covers more market conditions.

### Phase 2: replay the strategy with realistic fills

Every simulated trade pays:
- **Price impact:** the constant-product AMM formula on the pool's actual reserves at that moment, or the pump.fun bonding curve.
- **Fees:** the DEX fee (pump.fun about 1%, AMMs about 0.25%), Solana priority fees and Jito tip, and any token transfer tax.
- **Delay:** entry 1–3 blocks after the signal, plus a measured rate of failed transactions.
- **Honeypot failures:** sells that fail. A token you can't sell is a total loss, not a missing data point.

Then I compare against **controls**:
1. Random entry on all new tokens.
2. Random entry on tokens that **pass** the hard filter (this isolates the value of the filter).
3. Filter + Jev + Grok (the full system).

Each layer has to beat the one before it out of sample, and pass the same significance checks AlgoEdge uses. For overlapping trades, the significance verdict is read before the profit factor.

**What gets reported per configuration:** P&L in dollars, profit factor, Sharpe and Sortino, win rate, max drawdown, median hold time, worst single trade, and the share of trades that hit a −80% rug.

### Phase 3: paper trading (2 weeks)

The live pipeline runs end to end with simulated fills at real quoted prices. This checks that what the backtest assumed is what actually happens: latency, failed transactions, slippage.

### Phase 4: tiny live trading (only if Phase 3 passes)

- A burner wallet holding **$50–100**.
- Fixed $5–10 per trade.
- A hard daily loss limit.
- The goal is to measure real fills against paper. It is not meant to make money.

**What passing means:** the full system beats "random entry on filter-passing tokens" out of sample, after realistic costs, with a significant result, in at least two separate weeks.

If it doesn't pass, you get the same plain report, and nothing goes live.

## 4. Architecture

A separate service, not inside AlgoEdge. It's a different chain and has a different risk profile. It can report into the same Telegram channel.

```
[Helius websocket: new pools]──┐
[GeckoTerminal / DexScreener]──┼─> Detector ─> Hard filter (RugCheck + RPC + sell simulation)
                               │                  │ ~90% rejected, the reason stored
                               │                  v
                               │            Enricher: holders, creator history, liquidity, momentum
                               │                  v
                               │            Grok SOCIAL check (survivors only), stored with reasoning
                               │                  v
                               │            Judge ("Jev"): score + yes/no, stored
                               │                  v
                               └───────────> Risk: size, daily loss, max open, kill switch
                                                  v
                                            Executor: Jupiter / pump.fun + Jito, paper or live
                                                  v
                                            Position watcher: liquidity-pull exit, TP/SL, max hold
Store: SQLite at first, Postgres if it grows. Every decision and every reason is recorded,
so the backtest and live runs read the same table.
```

**Technologies:**
- Python 3.12, asyncio, `solana-py` / `solders`.
- `websockets` and `httpx`.
- SQLite or Postgres.
- `pandas` / `numpy` for the replay.
- Jupiter swap API, the Jito block-engine API, the RugCheck API, the GeckoTerminal / DexScreener APIs, and the xAI (Grok) API.

**Hosting:** a small Linux VPS, about 2 vCPU and 4 GB, placed near Solana's validator clusters (Frankfurt, Amsterdam or New York). The current Windows VPS is the wrong place: shared with the live bot, and far from validators.

## 7. Honest expectations

- **Most new tokens go to zero.** The filter can only lower how often you hold one. It does not create an edge by itself.
- **The edge, if there is one, is speed plus selection,** and both get competed away. Bots on pump.fun are very fast.
- **The TikTok numbers come from a single token on a single night,** posted by an account promoting a "setup". That proves nothing either way. Phase 2's controls are what would.

## 9. Pilot follow-up (one hour later)

Re-checked at 18:01 UTC, about 57 minutes after the snapshot. Data: `pilot_2026-10-16_followup_1h.json`.

| Group | Tokens | Median change in value | Down 80% or more | Doubled or more |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Flagged as danger | 34 | **0.0%** | 0 | 1 |
| Clean | 53 | **0.0%** | 2 | 0 |

All 87 pools were still listed. Median liquidity change was +0.1%; 5 pools lost 80% or more of their liquidity.

**What this says:** the typical new pump.fun token **didn't trade at all in its first hour**. Its value and liquidity sat where they launched. The few that moved are too few to compare the two groups: 1 doubled, 2 collapsed.

**It tells us nothing yet about whether the filter works.** What it does settle is the test design:
- Outcomes must be measured over **24 hours or more**, not one.
- The detector must ignore tokens that never attract trades. Most of the 1,250 an hour are dead on arrival, so "first trade" or a volume threshold should start a token's clock, not its creation.
- A sample of 87 is far too small. Phase 1 collects thousands.
