## First, a correction I owe

I recommended the H4 Donchian basket at **+0.3023R, t +2.17, $15,975 on
$10,000**. That number is reproducible and I stand behind the arithmetic.
**I should not have recommended it, and here is why.**

| | |
|---|---|
| Median trade | **−0.322R** |
| Top 1 trade | **31.4%** of all profit |
| Top 3 trades | 56.2% of all profit |
| Top 10 trades of 281 | **104% of all profit** |

The other 271 trades collectively lose money. And the result is not stable
under a change that should not matter at all — when you are allowed to
re-enter after an exit:

| Re-entry rule | Trades | Expectancy | t |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| same candle as the exit | 281 | +0.3023R | **+2.17** |
| 1 candle later | 268 | +0.2501R | +1.74 |
| **2 candles later** | 261 | **+0.1548R** | **+1.06** |
| 3 candles later | 248 | +0.2011R | +1.32 |

An arbitrary implementation detail — one that no trader would consider part
of the strategy — moves expectancy by half and the t-statistic from
significant to nothing. That is what a result carried by ten trades looks
like when you poke it. **A system whose entire edge is ten trades out of 281
is not something to put money behind**, and I presented it with more
confidence than it earned.

Separately: `TrendBreakout_v1` **already exists in this codebase** and is the
same rule (Donchian + chandelier trail). My "new" H4 system is that strategy
with a 1-day channel on H4 bars instead of a 20-day channel on M15. Nothing
needed building.

## The scan

No strategy. A flat question asked of every bar:

> Given the market is in state S, what happens next, and how reliably?

**17,237 cells**: 10 real markets × 5 timeframes (M5, M15, M30, H1, H4) × 4
horizons × ~20 states × 5 bins. States are anything computable from past bars
only — candle shape, consecutive same-direction runs, position in the recent
range, volatility regime, distance to the nearest round number, hour, day of
week, volume against its average.

### Two corrections without which the scan is worthless

My first run returned eighteen "edges" on gold, almost all **long** at 50–52%
hit rates. That was the five-year gold uptrend wearing a costume. Fixed:

1. **Drift is subtracted.** Every cell is measured as its excess over the
   unconditional forward return for that asset, timeframe and horizon. A
   state that merely rides a bull market now scores zero.
2. **Overlap is removed from the significance test.** A 12-bar forward return
   sampled at every bar gives twelve overlapping views of the same twelve
   bars; n looked like 16,000 when the independent count was ~1,300, inflating
   every t by about √12. The t-statistic is computed on a subsample taken
   every h bars.

Also fixed: a divide-by-near-zero on ATR warm-up bars that produced a return
of 1e11 and won the scan with a t-statistic of two billion.

### What the scan found

| Bar | Cells clearing it in **both** halves |
|---|---:|
| hit rate > 53% | 376 |
| hit rate > 55% | 73 |
| hit rate > 57% | 15 |
| hit rate > 60% | **0** |
| positive with t > +2 | **23** |

**21 of those 23 are the same finding**: state `dow`, bin `[-inf, 1.0]`,
always long, on GBP pairs, appearing at M5, M15, M30, H1 and H4 and at every
horizon. One effect, counted twenty-one times.

That bin is **Sunday**.

## The Sunday session on GBP pairs

Deriv quotes an FX session on Sunday. One observation per weekend — because
all the bars inside one Sunday evening are one event, not hundreds:

| Symbol | In-sample (180 wks) | Out-of-sample (77 wks) |
|---|---|---|
| **GBPJPY** | +0.3185R, t **+5.41**, hit **69.4%** | +0.4205R, t **+4.47**, hit **79.2%** |
| **GBPUSD** | +0.1880R, t +3.68, hit 59.4% | +0.4306R, t **+5.19**, hit **75.3%** |
| USDJPY | +0.2394R, t +4.52, hit 67.2% | +0.0782R, t +0.75 — fades |
| EURUSD | +0.1104R, t +2.02 | +0.2017R, t +1.99 — marginal |
| XAUUSD | −0.0270R | +0.2161R — inconsistent |

Pooled over all 257 weekends: GBPJPY **+0.3491R, t +6.99, 72.4% hit**.

That is a real statistical regularity, and it is the high-hit-rate profile you
asked for — not a trend system's 38%.

### Then it meets the spread

Deriv's Sunday spread is **2–3× its weekday spread**: GBPJPY 16.0 points on a
Sunday against 7.0 on a weekday.

With entry at the next bar's open and that spread paid:

| Symbol | In-sample | Out-of-sample |
|---|---|---|
| GBPJPY | +0.1153R, t +1.95 | +0.2205R, t +2.34, hit 67.5% |
| GBPUSD | +0.0022R, t +0.04 | +0.2394R, t +2.91 |

The spread takes roughly two thirds of it. **GBPUSD's in-sample half is flat**
— only the out-of-sample half works, which is a sign flip, not an edge.

### As an actual order, with a real stop

Entry at the open after the first Sunday bar, stop at a multiple of daily
ATR(14), flat after a fixed hold:

| Rule | In-sample | Out-of-sample | $10,000 at 1% risk | Max DD |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **GBPJPY 0.5×ATR, 24h** | +0.119R, t +1.31 | **+0.291R, t +2.12, hit 59.2%** | **$14,769 (+$4,769)** | **8.0%** |
| GBPJPY 0.75×ATR, 24h | +0.075R, t +1.18 | +0.215R, t +2.13, hit 61.0% | $13,223 (+$3,223) | 6.0% |
| GBPJPY 0.5×ATR, 12h | +0.023R, t +0.32 | +0.161R, t +1.45 | $11,553 (+$1,553) | 9.3% |
| GBPUSD, any | **negative in-sample** | positive | **$9,196–$10,256** | up to 24.9% |

**GBPUSD is rejected**: negative in-sample in all four configurations, and
three of them finish below the starting balance.

**GBPJPY 0.5×ATR / 24-hour hold** is the one candidate. 243 trades over five
years, about one a week, **$10,000 → $14,769 (+47.7%), 8.0% maximum
drawdown.**

## What I will not claim about it

* **The in-sample half is not significant** (t +1.31). Only the out-of-sample
  half is. That is the wrong way round and it means the effect may be
  strengthening, or it may be a 77-week run of luck.
* **It was found by scanning 17,237 cells.** It appeared 21 times, but that is
  one finding, and one finding out of 17,237 tries is roughly what chance
  produces.
* **FundedNext has zero Sunday bars.** This is Deriv-only and cannot be
  cross-validated on another broker's data at all.
* **The biggest unknown is not statistical.** Deriv quotes FX on Sunday while
  the interbank market is shut. Those are indicative prices on their own book.
  Whether you can actually fill at them, in size, repeatedly, without the
  broker restricting it, is a question no amount of historical data answers.

**The next step is not more backtesting.** It is placing this on demo for ten
consecutive Sundays and comparing the fills to the quotes. If the fills match,
there is something here. If they slip, there never was.

## What did not survive the scan

Worth recording, because these are the places people look:

* **No cell anywhere reached a 60% hit rate in both halves.** Out of 17,237.
* **Candle shape predicts nothing.** Body fraction, wick ratios, range against
  ATR — not one survived in either direction.
* **Consecutive-run length predicts nothing.** No "after three red candles"
  effect at any timeframe.
* **Round numbers predict nothing** once drift is removed, which agrees with
  this project's earlier finding that fading them is worse than fading a
  random price.
* **Going to lower timeframes did not help.** M5 and M15 produced no more
  surviving cells than H1 and H4 — they produced the same Sunday effect at
  higher frequency, and nothing else.
